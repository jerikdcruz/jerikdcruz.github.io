      <i>
        "The horizon, my ancestors say,<br>
        is an eye. When it shuts<br>
        its lashes sift the sea for rafts<br>
        and turn fishermen and divers to stone,<br>
        their hands still clenched around pearls,<br>
        their blood turning crystalline and cold.<br>
        <br>
        <br>
        The gods were in love with the horizon.<br>
        They hung their jewels<br>
        on the night-sky and ebbed into the eye<br>
        while my people stared<br>
        at the orphaned trinkets.<br>
        The priestesses rummaged<br>
        through their rucksacks.<br>
        They rubbed twigs and killed goats.<br>
        They chanted and chanted<br>
        until their voices felled the trees.<br>
        <br>
        <br>
        The gods have vanished.<br>
        The storms and smog have snatched<br>
        their jewels from the sky.<br>
        When the skies cleared, we saw a sun<br>
        not even ours."
      </i>
      <footer class="blockquote-footer">
        Mikael de Lara Co, <cite title="Source Title">Archipelago</cite>
      </footer>
    </blockquote>
